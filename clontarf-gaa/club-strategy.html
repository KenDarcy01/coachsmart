Our Club Strategy

Everything Clontarf GAA does with its players is built on the same foundation. Our ethos sets the standard for how everyone in the club treats one another. Our playing philosophy sets the standard for how our teams play the game. Club support from the GPO and DOS backs coaches up on the ground, so a single player pathway is delivered every week through consistent session plans, building the core skills every player needs. This page sets out how the pieces fit together, and breaks each one down into the detail coaches and mentors need on the sideline.

The Clontarf GAA club strategy, top to bottom

Non-negotiable, at every age group

Club Ethos

The ethos is the foundation everything else in this document sits on. It doesn't change by age group, code or team status — it applies to a Nursery session on a Saturday morning exactly as much as it applies to a Senior Championship match.

1Respect & Integrity

How we treat each other, our opponents and match officials, win or lose.

  • Referees' decisions are accepted — no dissent, from players, coaches or sideline.
  • Coaches model the behaviour they expect from players.
  • Mistakes are owned, not blamed on teammates, officials or opponents.
  • Opposition players and clubs are treated as guests and hosts, not enemies.

2Inclusivity

Every child who wants to play Gaelic Games in Clontarf has a place to do it.

  • No child is cut or turned away at underage level, regardless of ability.
  • Fair game time is protected, especially at Nursery through U12.
  • Sessions are adapted, not standardised, for players of different abilities.
  • Football, hurling/camogie and handball are all open doors into the club.

3Wellbeing

The player's welfare comes before the result, every single time.

  • All mentors are Garda vetted and Child Protection trained before they coach.
  • Training load and match minutes are managed with age and fatigue in mind.
  • Coaches check in on players, not just their skills — a listening ear matters.
  • A safe, positive environment is treated as a precondition for learning, not a nice-to-have.

4Passion & Pride

(for Cluain Tarbh)

A lifelong connection to the club, not just a season of it.

  • Pulling on the Clontarf jersey means representing the whole club community.
  • "Play and Stay" — we want players involved in the club for life, as players, coaches, volunteers or supporters.
  • Effort and commitment to the club badge are celebrated as much as scores and results.
  • Past players are encouraged back in as mentors — pride is passed down, not left behind.
How our teams play the game

Playing Philosophy

Once the ethos is in place, the playing philosophy describes the style of play we coach towards at every level — scaled to what's age-appropriate, but consistent in direction from Nursery to Senior.

1Attacking Mindset

Players are coached to look forward first. Possession is a platform to create, not just protect.

  • Positive first thought on the ball: can I advance play?
  • Risk-taking and creativity are encouraged, not punished when they don't come off.
  • Runners support the ball-carrier by offering forward options.

2High-Intensity Work-Rate

Effort off the ball is coached with the same care as skill on it.

  • Hustle back into position and support play at pace.
  • Physical conditioning is built progressively to sustain intensity for a full game.
  • At younger ages this means enthusiasm and hustle, not literal press-and-tackle systems.

3Skilful Execution

Technical quality is never sacrificed for outcome — "master the ball" starts on day one.

  • Two-sided competence is a target at every age: dominant and non-dominant hand/foot/side.
  • Skills are drilled to a standard, not just introduced and moved past.
  • Game speed execution is the end goal of every technical drill.

4Intelligent Decision Making

Reading the game is coached explicitly, growing from simple choices to full tactical awareness.

  • Coaches ask questions ("what were your options there?") rather than only giving instructions.
  • Game understanding builds from "where do I go" at U8 to positional and tactical roles by U15+.
  • Players are trusted to make the call on the pitch, and reviewed afterwards, not overridden mid-play.
Backing coaches on the ground

Club Support

The pathway and playing philosophy don't just live on paper — two dedicated club roles exist to help every mentor deliver them. Support from both comes the same way: coach-the-coaches sessions to build knowledge, and face-to-face session support pitchside, working alongside coaches during their actual training sessions.

GPOGames Promotion Officer

The club's dedicated resource for growing the games and supporting coaches week to week.

  • Runs coach-the-coaches sessions on the pathway, philosophy and specific technical content.
  • Provides face-to-face support at training, working alongside mentors in real sessions.
  • Helps coaches translate this strategy into practical, age-appropriate session plans.
  • First point of contact for any coach who wants extra support or guidance.

DOSDirector of Sport

Oversees the playing side of the club strategy end to end, across every code and age group.

  • Delivers coach-the-coaches sessions alongside the GPO to keep standards consistent.
  • Provides face-to-face session support, especially at key transition points on the pathway.
  • Keeps every team, from Nursery to Senior, aligned to the one ethos and philosophy.
  • Owns the overall coaching strategy and how it evolves season to season.
One player, one journey

Player Pathway

The pathway is organised into four levels. Each level builds directly on the one before it, and each is delivered through the same session plans framework, scaled to age, backed by club support from the GPO and DOS. For the full age-by-age technical curriculum — skills checklists, session content and coach characteristics for every age group — see the Coaching Pathway Mentor Handbook.

1 Nursery Ages 4–6 2 Skill Development Ages 7–10 3 Refinement Ages 11–15 4 Performance Ages 16–18 & Adult
1 Ages 4–6

Nursery

The starting point of the pathway. The only goal is that every child has fun with a ball in their hand or on a hurl, and starts to build the basic physical literacy every game skill depends on.

Focus
  • Fun as the number one priority, every session.
  • First touches: catching, striking, kicking, ball control.
  • Fundamental movement — running, jumping, balance, coordination.
  • A ball each, or between two, at every session.
What good looks like
  • High coach-to-player ratio (around 1:5) so every child gets attention.
  • Small-sided games only — 2v2, 3v3, 4v4 maximum.
  • Discovery and trial-and-error over instruction.
  • No standings, no scores kept — the outcome is enjoyment.
2 Ages 7–10

Skill Development

Players broaden their technical range across codes and start to combine skills under light pressure, while still trying every position and both sides of the ball.

Focus
  • Wider technical vocabulary: catching styles, striking variations, tackling basics.
  • Introducing zones and positional structure without locking players into one role.
  • Players try every position to build a full picture of the game.
  • Simple decision-making: look up, find space, use the ball early.
What good looks like
  • Small-sided games up to 6v6, still with plenty of touches per player.
  • Coaches guide with questions rather than direct instruction.
  • Individual skill practice sits alongside game-based learning every session.
  • Actual results stay secondary to development.
3 Ages 11–15

Refinement

Skills learned earlier are consolidated and applied under real pressure. Players start to understand team play, positions, and the tactical "why" behind decisions, alongside their physical development.

Focus
  • Sport-specific skills refined for consistency at pace and under contact.
  • Positional understanding: what does my role require of me, in and out of possession?
  • Introduction of game systems — using a sweeper, dealing with one, zone marking.
  • Structured athletic development: speed, strength and conditioning appropriate to age.
What good looks like
  • Game-based scenarios with a tactical theme, not just open small-sided games.
  • Players are asked to reflect on decisions after training and matches.
  • Only qualified coaches deliver strength and conditioning work.
  • Session length grows with age, up to roughly 60–70 minutes.
4 Ages 16–18 & Adult

Performance

The pathway's destination. Players fine-tune skills and performance, then train to compete and maximise performance, whether that's on a Minor, U20 or Senior team — and the club works to retain them beyond their playing days.

Focus
  • Fine-tuning technique and decision making at full match intensity.
  • Full tactical literacy: game plans, opposition analysis, in-game adjustments.
  • Individual preparation — lifestyle, recovery and conditioning becomes the player's own responsibility.
  • A clear route from Minor/U20 panels into Senior football, hurling, camogie and ladies football.
What good looks like
  • Session content driven by team and individual performance goals.
  • Players retained in the club as mentors, coaches or volunteers once playing days end.
  • In-depth tactical awareness applied by players themselves, not just directed by the sideline.
  • The same core ethos — respect, inclusivity, wellbeing, pride — still holds at Senior level.
How every session is built

Session Plans

Every level of the pathway is delivered through the same four building blocks, run in this order in a well-structured session. What changes with age isn't the framework — it's the numbers, the constraints and the complexity layered on top.

1Game-Based Scenarios

Conditioned games built around a specific tactical theme — a constraint, an overload, a system — that bridge pure skill practice and the real match.

  • Examples: playing with/against a sweeper, zone vs. man marking, creating and using overloads.
  • Introduced from Refinement level (roughly U12) as tactical understanding develops.
  • Coaches use questioning and reflection to embed the "why", not just the "what".

2Small Sided Games (SSGs)

Reduced numbers — 2v2 up to 6v6 depending on age — so every player gets far more touches and far more decisions than they would in a full-sided game.

  • The default format for training at Nursery through underage.
  • Maximises repetitions of the skills coached that week.
  • Naturally scales up in numbers and pitch size as players get older.

3Core Skills

Rotation-based technical stations — striking, catching, kicking, tackling — that drill fundamentals with a high number of repetitions per player.

  • Handling, striking, ball control and athletic movement all get dedicated station time.
  • Used to introduce and groove a new skill before it's tested inside a game.
  • Scales from basic ABCs (agility, balance, coordination) at Nursery to sport-specific technical work by Refinement.

4Athletic Development

Age-appropriate physical development — speed, strength, stamina, flexibility and coordination — that underpins every skill above it.

  • Fundamental movement and ABCs (agility, balance, coordination) at younger ages.
  • Structured speed, strength and conditioning work introduced from Refinement level.
  • Only qualified coaches deliver dedicated strength and conditioning sessions.

What every session plan is building towards

Skills

These are the core technical skills every level of the pathway and every session plan is ultimately building towards, across both codes.

Kicking

Striking the ball with the boot — placed, off the ground and from the hand, both feet.

Hand-Passing

The quick, accurate pass off the hand that keeps football moving at pace.

Striking (Hurling/Camogie)

Striking the sliotar with the hurl — off the ground and from the hand, both sides.

Soloing

Carrying the ball at speed while staying legal — the football solo and the hurling/camogie hop and solo.

High Catching

Winning and controlling the ball in the air under pressure, in both codes.
