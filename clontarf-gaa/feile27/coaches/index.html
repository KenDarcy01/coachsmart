Coaches' guide

Coaching the road to Féile

Five teams, three football and two camogie, all working to one plan. Féile football is the weekend of Saturday 10 April and camogie the weekend of Saturday 8 May 2027. This page is your part in it: what we need from you each week, how the girls develop, and how we keep them safe, happy and improving.

What we're aiming for

The same goals the families see

Ready for both weekends

Every girl able to play hard for every minute, and just as sharp for camogie in May as for football in April.

Injury-free

Activate warm-ups, strength and landing work at every session. Short, well-done reps beat long ones.

Skilful on both sides

Every girl improves on her own starting point, with her other side getting extra reps.

Still loving it in May

Enjoyment keeps girls in the game. If a session isn't fun, it isn't working.

Six phases

Each block a little harder than the last, then we ease off to arrive fresh for each Féile weekend

Phase 1

Foundation

25 Nov to 13 Dec

Learn every exercise properly. Build the habit of wall ball and easy running.

Phase 2

Christmas

14 Dec to 10 Jan

No team sessions from 21 Dec to 4 Jan; training is back on Tue 5 Jan. Short home sessions, with 24 to 27 Dec completely off.

Phase 3

Build

11 Jan to 28 Feb

More strength rounds, longer easy runs, skills from further out.

Phase 4

Competition prep

1 Mar to 28 Mar

Match-shaped running and skills when tired.

Phase 5

Football peak

29 Mar to 11 Apr

Easter camp 30 Mar to 1 Apr, then less work at the same speed. Two full rest days before Féile football.

Phase 6

Camogie peak

12 Apr to 9 May

Two easy days, two weeks of sharp camogie work, then the same taper. Two full rest days before Féile camogie.

Player development

Group-based, never personal

Superstar skills

Start with what each girl does well. Name it, and build on it.

Skills to develop

One or two things to work on, and how. Phrase them as abilities to grow, never as faults.

Fortnightly check-ins

From 27 January to 24 March, a quick look at how each girl is getting on, in football and camogie.

Kept private

Assessments stay with the coaches. Nothing with a girl's name goes on a public page or in a group chat.

Development groups turn this into home practice. There are four, run separately for football and camogie:

  • Ball skills: clean catching, passing and striking off both sides.
  • Speed & power: quick first steps and strong, safe landings.
  • Scoring: turning chances into scores.
  • Winning the ball: tackling, hooking and blocking to win it back.

Your assessment suggests the groups that would help a girl most. She picks them in the diary herself and gets a 10-minute drill for each one on her home nights.

Everyone does the same team plan. Groups are just a focus for practice at home, not a ranking and not a personal programme. Her choices are saved on her phone only, and the public pages never show who is in which group.

Looking after load

Two codes, school sport and growth spurts all add up

  • Ask what else she's doing. Many girls play school or other sports too. A girl with a heavy week does less, not more.
  • Growth spurts. Knee pain just below the kneecap, or heel pain after running, is common at this age. Take her off running and jumping for a week and let her parents know.
  • Pain that lasts, or any swelling. Stop, and point the family to a physio or GP. The physio's advice beats the diary.
  • Never make up missed work. A girl back from illness or a break picks up with today's plan, starting easy.
  • Her monthly cycle. Training is fine and often helps, but let her take it easier on harder days, and make it easy for her to say so.
  • Helmets for all camogie, every time.
  • Worried about how a girl is eating or feeling? Talk to the lead coach and her parents early, privately and kindly.

Benchmark checks (Sat 2 Jan, Sat 27 Feb, Sat 3 Apr) are for each girl to beat her own last score. Never rank them, read them out or compare girls.

How we talk to the girls

They're 14. Words stick

Everything we say or write about a girl, or to her, is positive and about what she can do. Praise effort and improvement. Correct the skill, not the player.

Food is fuel and energy, nothing more. We never talk about weight, calories or diets, and we never comment on how a girl looks. The Fuel for Féile guide shows the tone.

Instead ofSay
"Get fitter""Be able to last a full match"
"Weak side""Your other side, the one we're building"
"You're slow""Let's work on your first two steps"
"Eat less rubbish""Fuel up so you've energy for the second half"
